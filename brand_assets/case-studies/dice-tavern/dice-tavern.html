The Problem

The Dice Tavern makes laser-cut MDF terrain for Warhammer 40K. They started on Etsy and it took off. Really took off. But success on Etsy comes with a catch: the platform was taking roughly 30% of every sale in fees. As volume grew, that 30% became a serious amount of money walking out the door.

They needed their own website. But they're not technical. They didn't know how to build a site, didn't know SEO, and didn't want to be relying on a developer every time they needed to change a product or run a promotion. They needed something they could actually manage themselves.

On top of that, Etsy gave them no way to follow up with customers after a sale. No email sequences, no review requests, no cross-selling. Every transaction was a dead end. The customer bought, Etsy took their cut, and that was it.

What We Did

We didn't just build them a shop. We built a full content management system, a bit like WordPress but designed specifically for e-commerce. The whole point was that they could run everything themselves: edit the website, manage products, create promotions, handle orders. No developer needed after handover.

Their Own Store

No more 30% fees to Etsy

We moved them off Etsy onto their own website with Stripe payments going directly to their bank account. Same products, same customers, but now they keep the full sale. The checkout handles postage calculation, discount codes, and the payment flow. That 30% they were losing? It's theirs now.

Content Management

They edit everything themselves

The backend is a full CMS. They add new products, change prices, update descriptions, manage stock levels, upload images, and edit the website content, all without touching code. They create discount codes, set up bundle deals, and manage their SEO settings from a single admin panel. It's their website, and they actually know how to use it.

Cross-Sell & Bundles

Smart upselling built in

When a customer adds terrain to their basket, the platform suggests a matching gaming mat or storage solution at a bundled discount. They set the rules themselves in the dashboard: which products trigger which suggestions, what discount to offer. Average order value goes up and they didn't have to do anything extra.

Email Automation

4 emails per order, zero effort

Every order triggers a sequence automatically: instant confirmation, dispatch notification with tracking, a check-in 5 days after delivery, and a review request with a discount code at 14 days. That review request is why they've got a 4.9-star rating. The whole thing runs in the background without anyone touching it.

The Result

The Dice Tavern went from paying Etsy 30% on every sale to keeping it all. They run the entire business from their own platform now. They edit the website, manage products, create discount codes, set up cross-sell bundles, process orders through a pipeline, and track everything from one dashboard. They didn't need to learn to code. They didn't need to hire a developer. The CMS we built lets them do everything themselves.

The automated email sequence is why they've maintained a 4.9-star rating. Customers get a confirmation, a dispatch notification, a friendly check-in, and then a review request with a discount for next time. Four emails per order, all automatic, all feeling personal. And the cross-sell engine bumps up order values without anyone having to think about it.

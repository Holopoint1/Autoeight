How to Pitch Each Service

AI & Automation

Pitch framework + script
Lead with the time saved

Studies show AI tools save the average knowledge worker 5 hours a week (McKinsey). For a team of 10 that's 2,600 hours a year. At UK average salary that's over £60k in recoverable time — annually, compounding.

Name a company they know

Klarna runs customer service with a chatbot that does the work of 700 people. JPMorgan's COIN AI reviews contracts in seconds. The technology is real and deployed — the only question is whether it's built for you or whether you're using someone else's.

Tie it to their pain

Ask: "What's the most repetitive thing your team does every day?" Then pitch automating that specific task. Avoid selling "AI" — sell "four hours of your receptionist's Tuesday back."

"You said your team spends about 10 hours a week on quote follow-ups. That's half a full-time person, doing admin. We'd build you a system that drafts and schedules those follow-ups automatically — you'd just approve or edit. Matrix TSL went from 29 tools to 1 with something similar and got 3 hours a day back per person."

CRM & Integration

Pitch framework + script
Lead with tool sprawl

Gartner research shows 70% of SMBs have too many disconnected tools. Every one is a login, a data silo, and a monthly bill. The cost isn't just the subscription — it's the hours lost copy-pasting between them.

Anchor the cost

"You're already paying for the tools. You're just not making them talk to each other." Frame integration as unlocking the value they already own, not adding another line item.

Use the Matrix TSL proof

"Matrix went from 29 separate platforms to 1 connected system. Their sales team got three hours a day back. Now tell me — how many logins does your team have to juggle?"

"Your CRM, email platform, accounts, and calendar — right now they probably don't share data. Quote goes to the CRM, invoice gets raised by hand, accountant re-keys it into Xero. We connect them so it flows end-to-end. Typical ROI is 6 weeks."

Sales Automation

Pitch framework + script
Lead with the 3× stat

Salesforce's State of Sales: reps using automation are 3× more likely to hit quota. HubSpot found automated nurture sequences generate 320% more revenue than broadcast email.

Focus on pipeline hygiene

Most SMBs don't lose deals because their product is wrong — they lose them because follow-up falls through the cracks. Automate the follow-up and you win deals the competition let slip.

Proof: Matrix TSL 10× outreach

"Same team, same hours, 10× the outreach volume. Not because they worked harder — because the system picked up the repetitive bits."

"Let's say you get 50 leads a month and follow up with maybe 30 properly. That's 20 deals walking out the door because nobody had time. We'd build a sequence that chases every single one — personalised, from your address, with your voice. You just take calls from the ones who reply."

Pitch framework + script

"No templates, no per-seat licences, you own the code." Most SMB sites are on Wix or Squarespace, paying £20–50/month forever, locked into their platform. Our builds are yours permanently.

Differentiate from templates

"A £200 template will get you online. A custom build will get you ranking, converting, and integrated with the rest of your business. Different league, different outcome."

"You've got a Wix site that looks OK, but the phone isn't ringing from Google. That's because Wix sites don't rank — they're templates, and Google treats them like wallpaper. TM Joinery had the same problem. Six weeks after we launched theirs, they were Page 1 for joiner Halifax, joiner Bradford, joiner Huddersfield. All free traffic, forever."

Learning Management Systems (LMS)

Pitch framework + script
Lead with control

Off-the-shelf LMS platforms (Teachable, Thinkific, Kajabi) charge £30–£300/month forever and take a cut of every sale. A custom LMS is a one-time build that you own outright.

Focus on the community angle

Learn With Lorna didn't just need course delivery — they needed a student community, progress tracking, and branded experience. Third-party platforms don't do that without looking like a rental.

"If you're selling courses, you're paying a platform a slice every month and every sale. That's fine when you're small — but once you scale, you're handing over real money to use someone else's UI. We build you your own. You keep the revenue, you keep the brand, you keep the student data."
Objection Handling
"It's too expensive"
"What's the cost of NOT doing it? Matrix TSL was spending thousands in manual hours every month. Our system paid for itself in 6 weeks. The question isn't whether you can afford automation — it's whether you can afford not to."
"We can do it in-house"
"You could. The question is whether your team's time is better spent building automation or doing the work they were hired for. We've seen companies spend 6 months trying to build what we deliver in 6 weeks."
"We tried automation before"
"Most automation fails because it's off-the-shelf and nobody customises it to how your team actually works. We study your process first, then build around it. That's why our stuff sticks."
"We're too small for this"
"Our best results come from teams of 5-15. Smaller teams feel the impact faster because every hour saved is a bigger percentage of your capacity. The Dice Tavern went from paying 15% marketplace fees to zero overnight with their own platform."
"AI will replace my people"
"We're not replacing anyone — we're removing the admin so your people can do the work they're actually good at. Klarna's bot took the repetitive tickets. Their humans still handle anything complex. Same idea for your team."
"Our industry is too specific"
"Every industry says that. Joiners, bars, teachers, manufacturers — we've built for all of them. Because we study your process first, the industry details end up baked into the system, not fighting against it."
Cold Email Playbook
Day 0 – Intro
Open with something specific
Reference something real about them. One pain point. One proof point. Ask one question. Under 100 words.
Day 3 – Proof
Share a relevant result
"We did this for [similar company], here's what happened." Pick the closest case study. Link metric to their pain.
Day 7 – Angle
Try a different pain point
First email led with time? This one leads with cost. Or vice versa. Shorter. 2–3 sentences max.
Day 14 – Break-up
Low-pressure close
"Looks like timing isn't right. If things change, here's where to find us." No guilt. No pressure. Leave the door open.

Rules: Under 100 words. No attachments. Sound like a person. Never "I hope this finds you well." Subject under 50 chars, lowercase. One CTA per email — always a question.

Brand Voice

How Autoeight sounds

Like explaining to a business owner at a pub. Direct. Conversational. Confident but not arrogant. Contractions, mix short and long sentences, lead with outcomes not features.

Rules: Use contractions. Mix sentence lengths. Lead with outcomes. No em dashes everywhere. No perfect parallel structures. No exclamation marks.

Banned words:

comprehensivestreamlinedrobustholisticempowerbespokecutting-edgeseamlesslyleverageutilizefacilitateinnovativesynergyparadigm
Don't write like this
"We leverage cutting-edge AI technology to deliver comprehensive, end-to-end automation solutions that empower businesses to streamline their operations."
Write like this
"We build AI systems that save your team hours every day. No fluff, just tools that actually work."

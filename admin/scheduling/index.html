The Story to Tell Your Prospect

Here's a question: how many emails does it take to book a meeting in your business? For most companies, the honest answer is five or six. "Are you free Tuesday?" "Not Tuesday, how about Thursday?" "Morning or afternoon?" "I'll need to check — can I come back to you?" By the time you've booked a 30-minute call, you've spent more time arranging it than the call itself takes.

"Scheduling is one of the most invisible time costs in a business — because it feels necessary, so nobody questions it."

The Hidden Scale of the Problem

In a 5-person team that each book 3–4 meetings a day, the scheduling overhead alone can consume 7–10 hours of collective time daily. That's nearly a full working day lost across the team, every single day, just to arrange when people will talk to each other.

Then add no-shows. When there's no automated reminder, 20–30% of meetings simply don't happen. The prospect forgets, the customer moves on, and someone has blocked out time that produces nothing. Over a month, that's days of capacity gone.

Analogy to use: "It's like booking a restaurant. You don't email the restaurant five times to find a table — you just go online, pick a time, and you're done. Your business should work the same way for every customer trying to book time with you."

What Changes With Automation

When a prospect wants to speak with you, instead of an email chain starting, they receive a single link. They see your live calendar. They pick a time. It's confirmed instantly. A reminder goes to their phone the day before and an hour before. That's the entire process.

No emails. No back-and-forth. No double-booking. No forgotten reminders. No no-shows. The time saving per booking is enormous — but across an entire team, the cumulative impact is transformational.

The Financial Outcome

A team member on £30,000 costs roughly £14.50 an hour. If they're losing 1.3 hours a day to scheduling, that's about £19 a day — approximately £4,750 a year of their salary going into email back-and-forth. For a five-person team, that's £24,000 a year. Gone. Into scheduling.

"The most expensive thing in your business isn't software or overheads — it's the hours your people spend on tasks that a £20-a-month tool can do automatically."

What to Emphasise

  • Speed: Meetings are booked in seconds, not a day of email chains.
  • No-shows: Automated reminders cut no-shows by 80–90% — protecting valuable meeting slots.
  • After-hours: Prospects can book at midnight — no one has to be awake to confirm.
  • Customer experience: A frictionless booking process sets the tone for the entire relationship.

York's small businesses

York runs on two economies. Most websites serve neither.

York's economy has a shape you don't see anywhere else in the north. Millions of visitors come through the city, and a big slice of its small businesses live off that footfall. Cafes and tearooms around the Shambles and Fossgate. Guesthouses and B&Bs on the roads into town. Restaurants and bars that fill up hard on race days at York Racecourse and go quiet in between. Then there's a second, less visible economy outside the walls: trades and home services covering Acomb, Huntington and Haxby, and office-based firms out on the business parks at Clifton Moor and Monks Cross.

Both halves share the same weak spot. A visitor deciding where to eat tonight searches on their phone, lands on a slow template site with no live menu and no booking link, and moves on to the next result. A homeowner in Acomb looking for an electrician picks whoever looks most organised online. Meanwhile the owner runs enquiries out of a shared inbox, chases deposits by hand, and guesses at which channel actually brought the booking in.

A properly built site fixes both problems at once. It ranks for the searches that matter, and it carries the admin behind them. Bookings and deposits taken inline, enquiries routed and answered quickly, customer details landing in a CRM, review requests going out on their own. That's the work we do for York businesses.

The template problem

Why template sites struggle in York.

Templates get you online in a weekend, and in York that's exactly the problem, because everyone else did the same thing. When a dozen tearooms near the Minster sit on near-identical Squarespace themes, nobody stands out and nobody ranks well. The page structure is generic, load speed is whatever the platform decides, and every useful integration hides behind another monthly app fee.

You also never own what you've built. The subscription keeps running whether it's race week or a wet Tuesday in January, and when you outgrow the platform your content doesn't come with you cleanly. For a York business that trades hard in season and plans past next year, renting the shopfront forever is the wrong deal.

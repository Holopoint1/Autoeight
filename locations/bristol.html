Bristol's indie estate agencies

A premium-leaning market with a creative streak.

Bristol property runs higher per square foot than most regional cities. Clifton sits at the top with Georgian and Regency stock that commands genuine London-adjacent pricing. Redland and Bishopston handle the professional family belt with tall terraces and gardens at a premium. Henleaze and Westbury-on-Trym are quieter but no less in demand. Push south across the bridge to Long Ashton and out to Portishead and you're in the commuter premium zone where prices have climbed steadily for a decade.

Indies own a real share of this market because buyers in Clifton expect knowledge and discretion the chains don't always deliver. But the big chain-focused CRMs don't fit a tight office, and spreadsheets buckle the moment a couple of high-value vendors stack up at the same time.

The middle option didn't exist. So we built it.

The problem

Why Bristol agencies are losing leads.

A Clifton vendor emails on a Friday afternoon expecting a Monday valuation. A Redland applicant DMs Instagram about a tall terrace. A landlord with three properties around Bishopston fires off a tenancy question to WhatsApp. A Portishead viewing request comes through the website overnight. Each channel sits in its own queue.

In a market where the average house price means each instruction matters more than it does up north, the second-follow-up failure compounds quickly. The Friday vendor goes to whoever called on Monday. It's not a sales problem. It's an inbox problem.

# Marketing Plan

**Autoeight** (name under review)
Alfie Dobson, Halifax
July 2026

*Companion to the business plan. This covers how I get in front of buyers. Kept basic on purpose: I have about 2 days a week for this, so the plan only contains things I will actually do.*

## 1. The goal

Meetings with Halifax business owners. That is the whole of it. I am not picking a niche up front because I do not yet know what will work. I will call companies across Halifax, get in the room with the owner, offer a free review of their website and how they show up on Google, and sell from there. The meetings and the CRM numbers will show me who buys and what they buy, and that is how the niche picks itself.

Target for the first 6 weeks: a full Halifax call list built, calls happening every week, and free reviews being delivered. Every call, meeting and outcome logged.

## 2. Who I am targeting

Companies in Halifax. All of them, any sector. The only filters:

- Small enough that the owner answers the phone or is in the building
- They have a website that could clearly be better, or no real web presence at all

The list comes from my scraping tool, Google Maps and local directories. Each company goes in the CRM with sector, phone number, website address and a one line note on the state of their site. Sector matters because in 6 weeks I want to see which sectors actually said yes.

Trades and estate agents stay on the list like everyone else. The research I did on them is not wasted: if either sector starts converting, the playbooks are ready.

## 3. The offer and the message

The way in is a free review of their website and their Google presence. Plain words, said on the phone or in the doorway:

"I run a web and IT business here in Halifax. I will take a proper look at your website and how you show up when people search Google, free, and come show you what could be better. Takes half an hour of your time, and you keep what I find either way."

Why it works as an opener: every business has a website or knows they should, everyone quietly suspects theirs is not good enough, and free plus local plus in person removes every reason to say no.

What I never lead with: AI, technology, jargon. The word is "I will show you what could be better", not "digital transformation".

## 4. The free review

What they get, so it stays consistent and quick to produce:

- A look at their website: does it look right, work on a phone, load fast, and actually ask for the enquiry
- A look at their Google presence: what shows up when you search their name and their trade in Halifax, Google Business profile, reviews
- Anything else I spot while I am in there: hours being wasted on admin a system could do, is noted honestly

Delivered in person in about half an hour, with a one page written summary left behind. It always ends with a recommendation and a price: usually a website job (£500 to £2,500), SEO work, or an automation fix if the conversation surfaced one. Owner in the room, always.

The review is the foot in the door. The relationship and the follow on work are the business.

## 5. Channels, in order of importance

1. **Phone.** My strongest skill and the main channel. Set calling blocks, straight to booking the review meeting.
2. **Walk ins.** Halifax is compact. Batch streets and trading estates, drop in, book the review on the spot or leave a card and call back.
3. **The review meeting itself.** It is the best marketing I have: half an hour in front of an owner, showing them their own website with fresh eyes.
4. **Email.** Air cover before and after calls, never instead of them. Short and specific to their site, nothing that smells of mass mail.
5. **Referrals.** Asked for at every review and every handover: "who else do you know round here whose website needs a sort out?" Halifax owners know each other.
6. **LinkedIn and content.** One short post a week, findings from real reviews with names removed. Slow burn recognition, books nothing on its own.

## 6. The weekly routine

Roughly 2 days a week, split like this:

- List building: 2 hours
- Calls and walk ins: 6 to 8 hours
- Reviews and follow ups: whatever the calls generate
- Content, one post: 1 hour, and it is the first thing cut in a busy week

Calls always survive. Never trade calling time for anything else on this list.

## 7. Sales assets needed

- The review checklist (so every review covers the same ground quickly)
- A one page leave behind template for the written summary
- A simple card or flyer for walk ins
- Case study after each finished job, with permission

The landing page and anything printed with the business name on it waits until the name is decided. Calls, walk ins and reviews do not need to wait.

## 8. Budget

Near zero in cash. Calls cost time, walk ins cost shoe leather, the tools already exist. The real budget is my hours, which is what section 6 protects. No ad spend until the numbers show what deserves it.

## 9. How I will know it is working

Tracked in the CRM from the first call:

1. Calls or walk ins per booked review
2. Review to paid job conversion
3. Which sectors said yes, to both the review and the job
4. Job to repeat work or retainer conversion

After 6 weeks: look at what sold and who bought. If a sector stands out, that becomes the focus and the niche playbooks come off the shelf. If none does, keep going broad; the review pipeline is a business in itself.

## 10. First two weeks

1. Build the Halifax list: scraping tool plus Google Maps, into the CRM with sector tagged
2. Write the review checklist and the one page leave behind template
3. Decide the name (blocks printed material and the landing page, nothing else)
4. Start calling

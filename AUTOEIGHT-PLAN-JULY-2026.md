## 1. Executive summary

Autoeight is a one man software and automation business run by me, Alfie Dobson, from Halifax. I build websites, CRMs, learning platforms and AI automation systems, and I can sell them myself over the phone and in person. Most developers cannot sell and most salespeople cannot build. I do both, and that is the basis of the business.

I have around 5 months of contracted income, which buys me the time to find a repeatable source of new business. I am deliberately not betting on a niche yet, because I do not know what will work. Instead the way in is simple: call companies across Halifax, get a meeting with the owner, and offer a free review of their website and how they show up on Google. From those meetings I sell website, SEO and automation work. Every call and outcome goes in my CRM, and after 6 weeks the numbers will show me who buys and what they buy. If a sector stands out, I specialise; the research I have already done on estate agents and trades is ready on the shelf for that moment.

The long term goal is to find what sells, turn it into a product, and charge monthly for it. Recurring income from a product rather than my hours.

I am not asking for funding. The plan is funded by my current contract work.

## 4. Market analysis

**The market is companies in and around Halifax.** Any sector, small enough that the owner answers the phone or is in the building, with a website that could clearly be better or no real web presence at all. I am starting broad on purpose: the meetings will tell me which sectors buy, and the CRM will hold the evidence. Two sectors I have already researched in depth sit ready if the numbers point at them:

**Sector research 1: trades companies (construction, joinery and similar).** A lot of them have poor or dated websites, and the people running them are not technical. Many are sole traders who will not spend much, but they post their mobile numbers publicly on Google, which makes them reachable by phone. I have built an AI scraping tool that collects these companies, so a call list already exists. The service fits them: a simple, well made site with strong SEO at a price a small firm can justify.

**Sector research 2: small independent estate agents (1 to 3 branches).** They lose leads overnight and at weekends, spend hours chasing solicitors, and retype the same details into portals, CRMs and spreadsheets. They are big enough to pay for a fix and small enough that the owner can decide in one meeting. Big consultancies ignore them.

The homework on this market is already done: most small agents run one of four CRMs (Alto, Apex27, Street or Rezi). Street, Rezi and Apex27 have open APIs I can plug into. Alto does not, so Alto shops get automations that work from their emails instead. Walking in knowing their CRM and what it can and cannot do is credibility no generalist agency has.

**Competition.** Generalist web agencies, DIY website builders, and the CRM vendors' own add ons. My edge in both markets is the same: I can get the owner on the phone or stand in their shop, and I can build fast enough to show a working demo instead of a slideshow.

**What I have not done yet:** built the full Halifax list. That is the first job: my scraping tool plus Google Maps and local directories, every company logged in the CRM with its sector tagged, so in 6 weeks I can see exactly who said yes.

## 5. Marketing and sales

Selling is my strongest skill, so the plan is built around calls and visits rather than adverts. The full detail lives in the marketing plan; the shape of it:

- **The list:** every company in Halifax I can reach, built from my scraping tool, Google Maps and local directories, logged in the CRM with sector tagged.
- **Phone first, walk ins second:** calling blocks each week to reach owners directly, plus batched walk in rounds. Halifax is compact and the owner is often in the building.
- **The offer:** a free half hour review of their website and how they show up on Google, done in person, with a one page written summary left behind. Free plus local plus in person removes every reason to say no, and because I can qualify people on the phone myself I do not need a fee to filter time wasters.
- **The rules that keep free worth something:** owner in the room, every review ends with a recommendation and a price, and the number per week is capped so delivery time is protected.
- **Working demos:** because I build fast, I can turn up with something made from the prospect's own material, for example a quick mock up of a better homepage. Their own business on the screen sells harder than anything I could say.
- **Email and LinkedIn:** support the calls, never replace them. One short piece of content a week from real review findings, names removed.
- **Referrals:** asked for at every review and every handover. Halifax business owners know each other.

## 6. Management and operations

**The founder.** My skills are telesales and in person sales, fast software development, and delivering complete systems: websites, CRMs, LMS platforms, AI and automation tools, and general IT. I have run every part of the pipeline myself, from cold call to shipped system.

**Capacity.** Matrix takes around 2 days a week and Dice Tavern around 1, which leaves roughly 2 days a week for building this business until Christmas. Fixed scope packages and templates keep delivery inside 1 to 3 weeks per project so one person can manage it.

**Delivery.** Audit or scoping call (recorded with consent), written report within 3 working days, project built in 1 to 3 weeks on my existing tooling, handover with a short walkthrough video, then the retainer offer. A case study is written after each successful project, with the client's permission.

**A rule I hold myself to:** no invented numbers anywhere. Any time or money savings quoted in a report come from the client's own figures given on the call.

## 7. Financial plan

**Current income.**

That covers roughly 5 months of living costs, which is the window this plan has to prove itself. Costs beyond my time are close to zero: hosting and tooling are already in place, so what a project earns is what I keep.

**The numbers I will track from day one in the CRM:**

1. Calls or visits needed to book one review. What a lead actually costs in hours.
2. Review to paid job conversion. The number the whole plan lives on.
3. Which sectors said yes. This is what picks the niche later.
4. Job to repeat work or retainer conversion. The path to recurring income.

## 8. Risks

- **The runway runs out.** Biggest risk. Mitigated by keeping Matrix and Dice Tavern going while testing, and by the 6 week test deadline so a failing route is dropped quickly rather than slowly.
- **Spreading too thin by going broad.** Going niche free is a choice, not an accident, and it has a deadline: after 6 weeks the CRM numbers get read and anything that stands out becomes the focus. Broad is for learning, not forever.
- **Free reviews attract time wasters.** The owner must be in the meeting, reviews are capped per week, and every review ends with a priced recommendation so the tyre kickers reveal themselves quickly.
- **Alto has no public API.** Alto shops get email based automations instead of deep integrations. A Houseful partnership might be worth pursuing later if Alto shops turn out to dominate the pipeline.
- **Scepticism about AI.** Sell the outcome (answered enquiries, chased solicitors, saved hours), show a working demo, never lead with the technology.
- **One person ceiling.** Fixed scopes, templates and sub 3 week builds keep delivery manageable. Retainers smooth income between projects.
- **The rebrand drags on.** A half changed name is worse than either name. Decide before building new sales assets, then change everything at once.
- **Compliance.** GDPR on client data and property misdescription rules on AI written listings. A human approves anything client facing, and data stays inside the client's own accounts where possible.

## 9. Milestones

**Weeks 1 to 2.** Decide the name. Build the Halifax list with sectors tagged. Write the review checklist and the one page leave behind template. Start calling.

**Weeks 3 to 8.** Calling and walk ins every week, free reviews being delivered with the owner present, everything logged in the CRM.

**End of August.** Read the numbers: what sold, who bought, which sectors stood out. If one does, specialise and pull the ready made playbook off the shelf. If none does, keep going broad and review again.

**To Christmas.** Deliver the jobs, collect 1 or 2 case studies and referrals, adjust prices to what actually closed, and start the first retainers.

**The year ahead.** Getting fully established may take a year and I do not yet know exactly what the long term product is. The aim is that by then, one offering has proved itself and is on the way to being productised: a website package for trades, an LMS for colleges, or an estate agent automation suite, sold repeatedly with monthly fees.

## Appendix: founder's notes

**On timing.** Getting the business going may take a year. I am not fully sure just yet what I will do in the long term. I do have a bit of work currently that should keep me going for 5 months, which should be enough time to drum up some more long term business. Ideally I would find something that works, whether it is a website solution or a software solution like an LMS for colleges, and productise it and charge monthly fees.

**On what I enjoy.** This is important, as we do our best work on the tasks we enjoy. Something that feels like a game to me and looks like work to others means I will be able to spend all day doing it without a problem. I really like understanding a business's or individual's problems and solving them with AI and IT. They say if you want to find what you should do in life, look back into your childhood to what you did and enjoyed then. I was always an IT nerd, so I think I am on the right path pursuing this.

**On why sales.** I started doing sales after reading a book or passage written by a CEO, maybe Richard Branson, who was saying that if you want to go into business or eventually run a business, then sales is an essential skill in everything.
